# Expectativas de gênero para leitura beta

Use this as a lens, not as a formula. Compare the manuscript with the promise
made by its own project context, and explain when an intentional deviation is
working or confusing. Never treat a genre convention as a grammar error.

## Ficção literária e drama familiar

Look for a distinctive interior or social tension, meaningful image patterns,
and emotional change that accumulates rather than being announced. Ask whether
silences and omissions create productive ambiguity or simply hide needed
context. Track which relationship changes from the opening to the end of the
read passage.

## Romance

Track the central relationship, attraction or attachment, obstacles, agency of
both people, and the emotional turn promised by the chapter. A quiet scene can
work when it changes the relationship or raises a concrete question. Do not
require a fixed number of plot beats.

## Mistério e suspense

Track the question the reader is trying to answer, the distribution of clues,
the cost of delay, and whether revelations are fair in retrospect. Note when
withheld information feels like suspense and when it feels like the viewpoint
character is being kept artificially uninformed.

## Fantasia e ficção especulativa

Track the clarity of the immediate rules, the cost of unusual abilities or
institutions, and whether world details arrive through action rather than an
unbroken explanation. Mark proper nouns or invented terms that prevent the
scene from being understood, while allowing deliberate strangeness.

## Ficção histórica

Track the relationship between period detail and character action. Flag a
possible anachronism only as a question for writer-research; do not claim a
historical fact without a source. Notice whether the setting affects choices,
work, language, and social risk rather than serving as decoration.

## Any genre: reader-check questions

- What question made me read the next paragraph?
- What changed by the end of the passage?
- Which person, object, or place did I picture clearly?
- Where did I need to reread, and why?
- What expectation did the opening establish, and did the passage develop it?

Use the answers as evidence for the six scores in the report. Keep the report
about one page unless the writer explicitly requests a deeper reading.
